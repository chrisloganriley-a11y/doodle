# doodle — design brief

*pretty good coffee, roasted in philly.* Version 1, November 2026.

## The business

doodle is a coffee cart and drop-ship coffee brand in Philadelphia. The coffee is white-labeled from a local Philly roaster. The cart is where people try it; the bag at home, bought at the cart or by subscription, is the business.

## Who it's for

Urban 28–40-year-olds who make coffee at home every morning. They buy specialty coffee but aren't coffee nerds, and they pick things partly by how they look on the counter. Secondary: gift buyers, and cafe regulars who first meet doodle at the cart.

Why this group: about 80% of coffee is made at home, and 25–39-year-olds drink the most specialty coffee of any age group (National Coffee Association, 2026). Philly's coffee scene is mostly cafes and hybrid shops; no one owns a playful coffee brand you take home.

## Positioning

**Specialty coffee without the snobbery, drawn by hand.** The playbook is a pantry brand people leave on display (Graza for olive oil) applied to the morning bag. The understatement in "pretty good coffee" disarms the coffee-nerd crowd and lets the coffee prove itself.

## Personality

Warm, unfussy, a little wry, handmade. Calm rather than loud. It should feel like a note someone left you, not like an ad.

- Is: handwritten, lowercase, generous white space, one ink, small moments of color.
- Isn't: kraft-paper serious, maximalist, cartoonish, techy, or anything that looks generated.

## The lineup at launch

| Roast | What it is | Best for | Drawing | Highlighter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *daydream* | rotating light-medium roast, a new coffee every few months | drip, pour-over | a bird in flight | blush |
| *the usual* | house medium blend, chocolatey | espresso on its own, or in a latte | a mug with looping steam | butter |
| *late show* | decaf | after dinner | a moon and star | lilac |

Tasting notes are placeholders until the roaster picks the beans.

## The identity in one paragraph

A white bag with one periwinkle ink. The logo is "doodle" written as a single continuous pen line with bouncy, connected letters. Each roast gets a one-line drawing and its name in lowercase Fraunces italic, underlined with one pastel highlighter stroke. Everything else is set small in DM Mono. Color only ever arrives the way a hand would put it on paper.

## Where it has to work

Bags (front and back label), the cart and cups, the website and subscription flow, Instagram, stickers, and an app/favicon icon.

## Decisions made along the way

- Name: doodle (Pretty Good Coffee was already used by a cart caterer and a roaster, so it became the tagline).
- Logo: single-line bouncy script, no underline or flourishes.
- Color: periwinkle on white, after trying multi-color, cream, tomato and full periwinkle bags.
- Accent: one highlighter underline per roast name, after pills, swashes and watercolor read as computer-made.

## Open items before printing

1. Trademark: have an attorney clear "doodle" for coffee (class 30) and cart services. Similar marks exist (COFFEE DOODLE DOO, OODLE COFFEE).
2. Illustrator: redraw the wordmark, the three drawings and the three highlighter strokes by hand as master files.
3. Roaster: confirm beans, tasting notes, price per pound and minimums.
4. Website address for the QR code.

## Success, first season

People who try a cup at the cart buy a bag, and bag buyers come back for a second. Track: bags sold per cart weekend, and the share of first-time buyers who reorder within six weeks.
